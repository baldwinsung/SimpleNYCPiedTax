## Rates and scope

The surcharge is based on **DOF market value — not assessed value.** See
[Market value vs. assessed value](#market-value-vs-assessed-value) below for
why that distinction matters and why it isn't optional detail.

It's also a **cliff, not a marginal bracket**: once a property's market value
crosses the applicable threshold, the single corresponding rate applies to
its **entire** market value — not just the portion above the threshold (this
is the opposite of how income tax brackets work, and easy to assume
incorrectly). Rates below are "Phase One" (FY2026-27 and FY2027-28); Phase
Two starts 2028-07-01.

**Class 1** (1-3 family homes), threshold $5M:

| Market value | Rate (applied to entire value) |
| --- | --- |
| < $5,000,000 | not subject |
| $5,000,000 – $15,000,000 | 0.8% |
| $15,000,000 – $25,000,000 | 1.05% |
| $25,000,000+ | 1.3% |

**Class 2** (co-op / condo units), threshold $1M:

| Market value | Rate (applied to entire value) |
| --- | --- |
| < $1,000,000 | not subject |
| $1,000,000 – $3,000,000 | 4.0% |
| $3,000,000 – $5,000,000 | 5.25% |
| $5,000,000+ | 6.5% |

Class 2's threshold is lower and its rates are higher than Class 1's in Phase
One because, per Sullivan & Cromwell's analysis, DOF currently values co-ops
and condos using a comparable-*rental* methodology that "generally produce[s]
lower values" than Class 1's comparable-*sales* approach — the higher rate is
meant to compensate for the systematically lower valuation base, not to
single out co-ops/condos. From **2028-07-01 (Phase Two)**, DOF switches
co-ops/condos to a comparable-sales methodology and all property types move
to a single unified $5M threshold with Class 1's rate schedule.

Other scope notes:

- Only applies if the property is **not** the owner's primary residence
- Estimates of the actually-affected count have varied across sources as the
  process has played out: DOF's own July guidance cited **~17,000** letter
  recipients (see [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md)); other analyses
  since have cited figures from ~10,000 up to ~31,000 depending on what's
  being counted (letters sent vs. properties over the raw valuation threshold
  before exemptions). One independent analysis of the July 24 roll found
  **24,222** of the 959,710 published records (~2.5%) at or above the raw
  valuation threshold — before any exemptions are applied, so this is an
  upper bound, not the final owing count. None of these figures are
  reproducible from the public roll — see below.
- Projected revenue: **$500M+/year**, per the NYC Comptroller's office

## Market value vs. assessed value

NYC property tax bills are normally calculated from **assessed value (AV)**,
not market value (MV) — AV is a fraction of MV, and that fraction differs
by tax class:

| Tax class | Assessment ratio (AV as % of MV) | Annual assessment increase cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Class 1 (1-3 family homes) | ~6% of market value | 6%/year, 20% over 5 years |
| Class 2 (co-ops, condos, rentals) | ~45% of market value | 8%/year, 30% over 5 years |

This is why a Class 1 home can carry a *nominal* tax rate around 20% and
still have a low effective rate (~1%) against its market value — the caps
and the low assessment ratio mean AV is often well below MV, sometimes for
years, especially for property that has appreciated quickly.

**The pied-à-terre surcharge does not use AV.** It's defined directly against
DOF's determined **market value**, precisely so the assessment caps above
can't be used to duck the surcharge threshold — a home genuinely worth $8M
today is tested against $8M (its market value), not against some
lagging, capped assessed figure that might still read as $3M.

**Why this matters for this repo:** the `FMV` column in `tc2.db` (Class 2 /
co-op-condo roll) is DOF market value, so it's the right column to test
against the $1M Class 2 threshold. **The TC1 source CSV itself has no
value/market-value column at all** — confirmed directly against the file
(`PARID,OWNER,HOUSENUM_LO,HOUSENUM_HI,STREET_NAME,APTNO`, nothing else) — so
`tc1.db` has none either, and this tool currently has **no way to filter or
sort Class 1 (house) rows by market value**, and therefore no way to
identify which Class 1 rows are even potentially near the $5M threshold from
this data alone. That's a genuine limitation of the published TC1 roll file,
not something this tool chose to omit.

## Does DOF publish exemption-filing data or an API? No.

Checked specifically for this. What exists:

- **The roll itself** (TC1/TC2 CSVs) — already what `fetch.sh` downloads.
  Valuation only, not exemption status. See
  [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md).
- **A browser eligibility tool**:
  `nyc.gov/site/finance/property/property-surcharge-eligiblity-tool.page`
  (linked from the `nyc.gov/npsurcharge` hub) — an interactive web page for
  an individual owner to check their own situation. No API, no bulk export.
- **A secure DOF account portal** for owners who received a letter, to upload
  supporting documents and track their own exemption application. Per-owner,
  authenticated, not public data.
- **No Open Data / Socrata dataset** was found listing exemption applications,
  approvals, denials, or filing status. Given this is individual taxpayer
  filing data, that's expected — it's the kind of thing DOF would have no
  obligation (and likely a legal bar) to publish in bulk.

**Bottom line for this project:** there is nothing new to build a fetcher
against. The roll (already handled) remains the only bulk public dataset;
exemption status is DOF-owner-private by design, same as the ~17k letter
list already documented in [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md).

## Related: privacy backlash over the public roll

Worth flagging since this repo builds the same kind of lookup tool over the
same data. Because the July 24 roll (and a city-run searchable version of it)
is bulk-downloadable and includes name + address + value for ~960k
properties — the overwhelming majority of whom don't owe anything — several
outlets and a filed lawsuit raised "aggregation" privacy concerns: individually
public records (name, address, market value) becoming a mass-searchable,
downloadable target list when combined. Three homeowners filed a legal
challenge arguing the process improperly puts the burden on nearly a million
residents to prove they *don't* owe a tax most were never actually assessed.

This project's existing [DISCLAIMER.md](../DISCLAIMER.md) and the "roll ≠ who
owes" framing in [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md) already cover the
substance of this concern (same underlying data, same misreading risk), but
it's useful context for why that framing matters beyond just accuracy.
