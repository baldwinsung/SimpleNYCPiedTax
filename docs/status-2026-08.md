A timeline of the pied-à-terre (non-primary-residence surcharge) tax, and an
answer to "does DOF publish exemption-filing data anywhere?" (short answer:
no).

## Rates and scope

- **1-3 family homes** valued **$5M+** (DOF market value): 0.8%–1.3% surcharge
- **Co-op / condo units** valued **$1M+**: 4%–6.5% surcharge
- Only applies if the property is **not** the owner's primary residence
- Estimates of the actually-affected count have varied across sources as the
  process has played out: DOF's own July guidance cited **~17,000** letter
  recipients (see [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md)); other analyses
  since have cited figures from ~10,000 up to ~31,000 depending on what's
  being counted (letters sent vs. properties over the raw valuation threshold
  before exemptions). None of these figures are reproducible from the public
  roll — see below.
- Projected revenue: **$500M+/year**, per the NYC Comptroller's office

## Does DOF publish exemption-filing data or an API? No.

Checked specifically for this. What exists:

- **The roll itself** (TC1/TC2 CSVs) — already what `fetch.sh` downloads.
  Valuation only, not exemption status. See
  [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md).
- **A browser eligibility tool**:
  `nyc.gov/site/finance/property/property-surcharge-eligiblity-tool.page`
  (linked from the `nyc.gov/npsurcharge` hub) — an interactive web page for
  an individual owner to check their own situation. No API, no bulk export.
- **A secure DOF account portal** for owners who received a letter, to upload
  supporting documents and track their own exemption application. Per-owner,
  authenticated, not public data.
- **No Open Data / Socrata dataset** was found listing exemption applications,
  approvals, denials, or filing status. Given this is individual taxpayer
  filing data, that's expected — it's the kind of thing DOF would have no
  obligation (and likely a legal bar) to publish in bulk.

**Bottom line for this project:** there is nothing new to build a fetcher
against. The roll (already handled) remains the only bulk public dataset;
exemption status is DOF-owner-private by design, same as the ~17k letter
list already documented in [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md).

## Related: privacy backlash over the public roll

Worth flagging since this repo builds the same kind of lookup tool over the
same data. Because the July 24 roll (and a city-run searchable version of it)
is bulk-downloadable and includes name + address + value for ~960k
properties — the overwhelming majority of whom don't owe anything — several
outlets and a filed lawsuit raised "aggregation" privacy concerns: individually
public records (name, address, assessed value) becoming a mass-searchable,
downloadable target list when combined. Three homeowners filed a legal
challenge arguing the process improperly puts the burden on nearly a million
residents to prove they *don't* owe a tax most were never actually assessed.

This project's existing [DISCLAIMER.md](../DISCLAIMER.md) and the "roll ≠ who
owes" framing in [about-the-roll.md](about-the-roll.md) already cover the
substance of this concern (same underlying data, same misreading risk), but
it's useful context for why that framing matters beyond just accuracy.
